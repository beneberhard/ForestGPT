Knowledge Card 1.1 
Title: Harvester Types and Processing Concepts 
Text: 
Harvesters are full harvesting machines that can perform the operations of felling, extraction, 
delimbing, bucking, and stacking; this is referred to as fully mechanized harvesting. Based on 
technical characteristics, harvesters are classified into mini, small, medium, and large 
harvesters. 
Depending on the technical solution used for the delimbing process, a distinction is made 
between single-grip and double-grip harvesters. The single-grip harvester performs all 
processing phases with the harvester head. The double-grip harvester has a felling head on the 
crane arm and a delimbing and shaping unit on the carrier vehicle and is hardly used in Central 
Europe anymore. 
Source: 
FHP, Harvester und Forwarder in der Holzernte, 3.Auflage (2015)

Knowledge card 1.2: 
 
Title: 
Wheeled and Tracked Harvester Systems 
Text: 
A general distinction is made between wheeled harvesters and tracked harvesters. Wheeled 
vehicles are economical and environmentally friendly on slopes with a gradient of up to 
approximately 35% under good conditions and up to 40%. They are the most common carriers 
for booms and harvester units due to their greater range of applications, smoother handling, and 
use on public transport networks. For more traction, anti-skid chains, bogie tracks, or special 
tank tracks can be fitted. Tracked harvesters move via a tracked undercarriage. The larger 
contact surface increases stability and reduces ground pressure, but there is a risk of the 
machine slipping sideways, especially on slippery surfaces. The tracks can be made of metal, 
hard rubber, or plastic. 
Source: 
FHP, Harvester und Forwarder in der Holzernte, 3.Auflage (2015)

Knowledge Card 1.3:  
Title: 
 Forwarder 
Text: 
Forwarders (assortment tractors, carrier tractors, skidders) are manufactured in a frame 
construction with articulated steering. The centrally located articulation joint also 
allows both vehicles to turn against each other within a certain safety range. Forwarders 
are hydrostatically driven and have 4 to 10 wheels or, in rare cases, a tracked 
undercarriage. Depending on the equipment class, cranes with a reach of 5-10 meters 
are used for loading. In fully mechanized timber harvesting with harvesters and 
forwarders, these must be coordinated in terms of off-road capability so that the 
forwarder is able to transport all assortments produced by the harvester.

Knowledge Card 1.4:  
Title: 
 combined machine Harvester and forwarder in one 
Text: 
One concept that has not yet become widely established is the so-called Harvarder or 
Forwester, which can perform felling, processing, and skidding in a single operation. 
Recently, repeated attempts have been made to use such machines in energy wood 
production. However, it has generally been found that the productivity of harvesting 
chains with separate machines is higher than that of combination machines. The higher 
purchase price, especially for the forwarder function, significantly increases the total 
harvesting costs. Nevertheless, such combination machines offer an advantage in 
applications with low mass accumulation, as in this case, cost-effective transfer is only 
required for one machine. 
Source: 
FHP, Harvester und Forwarder in der Holzernte, 3.Auflage (2015)

Knowledge Card 1.5:  
Title:  
Control of the carrier vehicle Hydrostatic power transmission 
Text: 
Harvesters and forwarders usually have a hydrostatic power transmission system. The 
direction of travel can be changed under load and, like the travel speed, is controlled by 
simply tilting the drive control lever forwards or backwards. Differentials are usually 
equipped with differential locks and are also controlled electro-hydraulically by the 
driver. To save fuel, newer machines are equipped with a Load-Sensing-System, a 
hydraulic power control system that adjusts both the pressure and the volume flow of 
the pump to the conditions required by the consumer. This optimization allows smaller 
engines to be used. If the Load-Sensing-System is not available, the machine should be 
operated at as constant a speed as possible, which is usually around the average 
between the speed required for maximum torque and that required for maximum power. 
Source: 
FHP, Harvester und Forwarder in der Holzernte, 3.Auflage (2015)

Knowledge Card 1.6:  
Title:  
Measurement and control systems 
Text: 
A reliable measuring and control system is the most important prerequisite for efficient 
and value-optimized shaping with the harvester. Modern devices have a computer-
aided, programmable measuring and programming system for cutting to length and 
automatic trunk processing. It consists of the main computer, the screen, the measuring 
devices on the full harvesting unit, and the arm support controls. The operator programs 
the automatic measuring and shaping system according to the specifications of the 
forest owner or timber buyer. The length and diameter of logs or log sections are 
determined during the processing operation by measuring devices in the unit. The 
computer system processes the measurement data and controls the cutting unit, 
optimizes the assortment lengths, and prevents the shaping of unwanted assortments. 
Source: 
FHP, Harvester und Forwarder in der Holzernte, 3.Auflage (2015)

Structured summary: 
Fully mechanized timber harvesting is based on the coordinated use of harvesters and 
forwarders. Harvesters are multifunctional machines capable of felling, delimbing, 
bucking, extraction, and stacking, and are classified into mini, small, medium, and large 
types. Technically, a distinction is made between single-grip and double-grip harvesters, 
with single-grip systems dominating modern operations. Harvesters can be wheeled or 
tracked, where wheeled machines are more common due to versatility, smoother 
handling, and suitability for transport networks, while tracked systems offer higher 
stability and lower ground pressure. 

--- PAGE 9 ---
Forwarders are hydrostatically driven transport machines with articulated steering, 
cranes of 5–10 m reach, and wheels or tracked undercarriages. Their off-road capability 
must be coordinated with harvesters. Combination machines such as Harvarders 
integrate harvesting and forwarding in one unit but generally show lower productivity and 
higher costs. Efficient operation is supported by hydrostatic drives, load-sensing 
systems, and advanced measuring and control systems that optimize cutting accuracy, 
assortment formation, and value-optimized timber processing.

Title: 
Components of the Chainsaw Chain 
Text: 
The chainsaw chain consists of six main parts. These include the drive link, the right 
cutting tooth with depth gauge, the left cutting tooth with depth gauge, the connecting 
link without rivets, and the safety drive link. Together, these components form the 
functional structure of the saw chain and enable power transmission, guidance on the 
guide bar, and the cutting process. The alternating arrangement of right and left cutting 
teeth ensures continuous cutting action during operation.

Knowledge Card 2.2 
Title: 
Structure and Function of the Cutting Tooth 
Text: 
The incisor consists of the actual cutting part and the depth limiter. The cutting part is 
coated with a special chrome steel alloy, while the depth limiter determines the working 
depth of the cutting part. As the tooth tapers towards the rear, the chip channel 
becomes narrower, which facilitates chip transport. The planer tooth chain works 
similarly to a hand plane: the roof edge lifts the chip from the bottom of the kerf, while 
the breast edge cuts the chip off at the side. The depth of penetration and chip thickness 
are determined by the depth gauge and its clearance to the tooth roof.

Knowledge Card 2.3 
Title: 
Cutting Performance, Wear, and Tooth Shapes 
 
Text: 
The tooth crown and tooth face are coated with a thin chrome layer that forms the 
cutting edges. The correct and uniform thickness of this layer is crucial for cutting 
performance and service life. The tooth base forms the running surface on the guide rail 
and is exposed to high frictional stress, requiring a very wear-resistant base material. To 
prevent jamming in the kerf, the tooth top and tooth breast taper towards the rear and 
form a clearance angle, which is retained even after resharpening. To improve cutting 
performance, different tooth shapes have been developed, with the most common 
being the semi-chisel tooth with a rounded transition and the full chisel tooth with an 
angular transition between side plate and tooth crown. 
Source:

Knowledge Card 2.4:  
Title:  
Carburetor 
Text: 
In the carburetor, the gasoline is carried along by the air flow sucked in by the engine and 
finely atomized and mixed with the air, creating a combustible mixture for every speed 
and load. In the carburetor, the air flow is sucked in by the engine piston during the 
intake stroke. The speed of the air flow is increased by the cross-sectional narrowing of 
the streamlined air funnel (Venturi tube). The fuel is carried along by the air flow, 
atomized, and mixed with the air flow in the mixing chamber. Fine atomization is 

--- PAGE 13 ---
achieved by foaming the fuel into a fuel-air mixture via the air supply through the air 
nozzle below the fuel level. The throttle valve controls the amount of fuel-air mixture, 
thereby changing the engine power and speed. A general distinction is made between 
single carburetors, slide carburetors, and diaphragm carburetors, which are mainly used 
for chainsaws. 
Source:

Knowledge Card 2.5:  
Title:  
chain sharpening 

--- PAGE 14 ---
Text: 
If the cutting performance of the chainsaw chain deteriorates, you should be able to file 
it freehand at your workplace without any additional tools. With normal wear and tear, a 
few strokes of the file are sufficient. It is better to file the chain often, but only a little at a 
time, because a blunt chain results in poorer cutting performance, leads to laborious 
work, and damages the chain and the engine. As a general rule, sharpening is done with 
a two-handed grip. Depending on the chain type and wood, the filing angle is 25° - 35°. 
The cutting teeth are filed away from the body with even strokes. Always file every 
second tooth. It is important that all teeth are the same length. Once all the cutting teeth 
on one side have been sharpened, repeat the process on the other side. There are also 
filing gauges and aids available to make this process easier. 
Source: 
Slovik S., Lehrskript für den 2.Jahrgang Forst- & Arbeitstechnik, HBLA Bruck/Mur

Knowledge Card 2.6:  
Title:  
Maintenance of chainsaws 
Text: 
The more complex a machine or device is, the more care and maintenance it requires.  
Most damage to sprockets and blades is caused by insufficient chain tension. It is 
therefore necessary to check the chain tension regularly. A chain is correctly tensioned 
when it does not sag at the bottom of the blade and can still be pulled with two fingers. 
After five to six working days, the saw requires urgent maintenance. In addition to daily 
maintenance, the cooling air passages and cooling fins on the cylinder should be 
cleaned. Furthermore, the burrs on the chain running surfaces should also be filed off. 
The deflection star also needs to be greased. Especially with new saws, it is important to 
tighten any screws that are visible from the outside. The clutch bearing is normally 
greased via the crankshaft. Fuel used for cleaning must be disposed of properly. 
Source: 
Slovik S., Lehrskript für den 2.Jahrgang Forst- & Arbeitstechnik, HBLA Bruck/Mur

Structured summary: 
Chainsaw efficiency depends on the interaction of chain components, cutting tooth 
design, fuel preparation, sharpening, and maintenance. The chainsaw chain consists of 
drive links, cutting teeth with depth gauges, connecting links, and safety drive links, 
enabling power transmission and guidance on the guide bar. The cutting tooth, or 
incisor, includes a chrome-coated cutting part and a depth limiter that controls chip 
thickness and penetration depth. Cutting performance and service life are influenced by 
chrome layer quality, wear-resistant materials, and the clearance angle, which prevents 
jamming even after resharpening. 
Different tooth shapes, such as semi-chisel and full chisel teeth, are used depending on 
performance requirements. Engine performance relies on carburetors that mix fuel and 
air using airflow acceleration in the Venturi tube. Regular chain sharpening at correct 
angles is essential to maintain performance and avoid excessive wear. Maintenance, 
especially correct chain tension, lubrication, cleaning, and periodic servicing, is crucial 
to prevent damage to the chain, sprockets, blade, and engine. 
 
 
Knowledge Card 3.1 
Title: 
Purpose and Structure of the Felling Plan in steep area 
Text: 
The felling plan is intended to make the processing and removal of timber safer, easier, 
more gentle on the forest stand, and more economical. It includes determining the 
felling direction and felling shape in relation to the removal and processing of the felled 
timber. The felling plan also specifies the work progress and provides instructions 
regarding clearing the felling area and preparing bundles. The configuration of the timber 
is essentially determined by the planning method, which can be based on the tree 
method, trunk method, trunk section method, or assortment method.

Title: 
Transport Limits, Skidding Methods, and Weight Calculations in steep area 
Text: 
Depending on the capacity of the cable system, the maximum transport length, the 
maximum transport weight, and the corresponding configuration lengths must be 
specified in detail. Clear-cutting favors skidding in tree or trunk length using the head-
high method. For thick-stemmed trees skidded in tree or trunk length, the specific 
weight plus the weight of the branches must be calculated in solid cubic meters in bark. 

--- PAGE 18 ---
If the maximum transport weight of individual pieces is exceeded, skidding is carried out 
in half-lengths or trunk pieces are cut off. For thin-barked trees felled using the trunk 
method, one-third of the piece weight is used for sag calculation, while in the tree 
method half of the piece weight is used because branch weight must be considered.

Work processes

Text: 
There are two types of work processes: organizational and technical. Both types of work 
processes are determined by the work organization, with the technical process being 
part of the methodological work and the organizational process being part of the 
temporal and spatial sequence of the individual subtasks. The selection and application 
of the appropriate workflow is important for optimal work performance as well as for 
analyzing work processes and improving, developing, and specifying new work methods. 
The aim of designing the organizational workflow is to complete the work order with the 
least amount of time, cost, and effort, while ensuring maximum work safety and the 
appropriate use of equipment.

Knowledge Card 3.4:  
Title:  
Detailed development 
Text: 
The detailed development of the stands makes it possible to get close to the timber to 
be transported with the respective skid trail. This means that, depending on the 
transport method, the timber can be transported to the haul road in a way that is gentle 
on the stand, gentle on the ground, cost-effective, and with a low risk of accidents. The 
fine development of the usable areas in the cable yard is carried out using cable routes. 
Sometimes it is necessary to create cable routes with access lanes. 
A cable route is a straight strip of land through a stand, cleared area, etc., through which 
the carrying cable of a mast cable device is guided. The width of this route depends on 
the skidding method and is approximately 2-5 meters wide. 
Source:

Accident prevention through work organization 
Text: 
Accidents can be prevented through work organization by specifying optimal fine 
development, strike order, group size, well-considered work processes, and continuous 
positive performance monitoring. Compliance with the project-based execution of the 
rope system, or proper execution according to work instructions or nature's 
specifications. Support positions, support heights, dimensioning of supports and 
anchors. Compliance with buckling and spread angles when anchoring backrest devices 
and cross cables. Professional cable system. Checking the maximum permissible 
operating tension and the basic tension, as well as the tension of heavily stressed guy 
ropes and cross cables. The rope tension can be determined approximately using the 
rope swing method. Aviation obstacles arising in connection with the construction of a 
forest cableway system must be reported to the local governor, who is also responsible 
for granting exemptions.

Title: Economically optimal timber-harvesting method 
(“Best Method”) 
 
Text: 
The concept of the economically optimal timber-harvesting method refers to the 
selection of a harvesting system that is technically feasible, compliant with safety and 
environmental standards, and minimizes total harvesting and transport costs. In the 
Swiss National Forest Inventory (NFI), this “best method” is determined for each sample 
plot by combining spatial data, stand characteristics, and expert-based decision trees.

slope, distance, and accessibility conditions. For each feasible system, harvesting costs

comparison with the currently applied method. Results show that, from an economic 
perspective, higher levels of mechanisation are often preferable and could replace 
motor-manual systems on many sites. However, the best method is not automatically 
applied in practice due to structural constraints such as small enterprise size or the 
need to utilize existing machinery and personnel.

KNOWLEDGE CARD 5.1 
Title: Slit-cut method as a regeneration-oriented 
harvesting system 
 
Text: 
The slit-cut method is a silvicultural harvesting system developed for the regeneration of 
mountain forests without clear-cutting. It is based on small, elongated openings (“slits”) 
arranged alternately along cable yarding corridors. The length of a slit corresponds 
approximately to one mature tree length, while its width is limited to about half a tree 
height. Slits are positioned according to tree diameter, stand structure, and existing 
regeneration patches. This spatial arrangement creates heterogeneous light conditions 
that promote natural regeneration of mixed species, particularly spruce, fir, and beech. 
From a harvesting perspective, the slit-cut method enables timber extraction while 
maintaining continuous forest cover and protective forest functions. The method is 
specifically designed for steep terrain, where regeneration interventions must be small-
scale, controlled, and compatible with cable-based extraction systems.

Title: Tree-length method in cable yarding operations 
 
Text: 
In the tree-length method, trees are felled in the stand and extracted as whole stems 
using cable yarders, while processing and measurement are carried out at the landing. 
This method replaces the assortment-based system in steep terrain, where processing 
in the stand is labor-intensive and inefficient. The tree-length method reduces the 
number of hooking operations and shortens cycle times, especially under winter 
conditions with snow cover. Because stems maintain ground contact with the tree top 
along the corridor, stable guidance during yarding is achieved. Integration of processors 
at the landing allows continuous flow of timber and immediate sorting according to 
market requirements. The method is particularly well suited for mechanized cable

Forest road construction with the hydraulic hammer 
Text 
Before the wood in a stand can be harvested, it is important to get a road through the 
forest. In most cases, this is done with an excavator and an attachment in the form of an 
excavator bucket. This Knowledge Card focuses speciﬁcally on the hydraulic hammer. 
The hydraulic hammer is used to break up coarse, stony material. The device is used 
when the buckets are no longer economical due to the composition of the material 
being processed. The machine works on the impact principle.  This means that the 
system requires between 100 and 180 liters of oil per second for an 18- to 26-ton 
excavator. This is needed to build up pressure in the hammer cylinder in order to move a 
piston. The energy that builds up is released by means of a control valve and the chisel 
at the tip strikes the rock. During operation, an impact frequency of 360-650 blows per 
minute is achieved. The most important thing with this tool is good storage of the chisels 
and suƯicient lubrication.  
In the forest, it is used where the route is rocky and progress is only possible by breaking 
up the rocks. Building a road with such material takes longer, but it has the advantage 
that suitable rock is already on site and very little or nothing needs to be transported 
from a quarry to the construction site.

Terrain and stand characteristics have a decisive influence on the selection of appropriate 
forest harvesting systems. Slope gradient, soil conditions, and accessibility largely
